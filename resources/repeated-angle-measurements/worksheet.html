Repeated Angle Measurements

Interactive worksheet · Bachelor level · ~60 min

1 · Theory in brief

When the same angle is observed n times under equal conditions, the best estimate is the arithmetic mean. The spread of the residuals tells us about the precision of a single observation; dividing by √n gives the precision of the mean.

x̄ = Σ xᵢ / n     vᵢ = xᵢ − x̄     σ = √( Σ vᵢ² / (n − 1) )     σx̄ = σ / √n

Check: Σ vᵢ must be ≈ 0. Observations with |vᵢ| > 3σ are flagged as possible outliers (to be investigated, not deleted automatically).

2 · Compute with your own data

Enter one reading per line, in degrees minutes seconds separated by spaces (e.g. 47 23 15.4) or decimal degrees.

3 · Check yourself

Using the dataset above, compute by hand or with a calculator the standard deviation of the mean σx̄ (in arc-seconds, 1 decimal) and enter it below.

4 · Questions for discussion

  1. How many repetitions are needed to halve σx̄? What are the practical limits of this strategy?
  2. Does averaging reduce systematic errors (e.g. collimation error)? How do face-left/face-right observations help?
  3. Add a reading wrong by 20″. How do the mean, σ and the outlier flag react?
